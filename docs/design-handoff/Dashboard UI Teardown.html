KRNL Client Dashboard — UI teardown

A complete picture of what exists today, for a redesign. Written 1 August 2026. Everything below is taken from the live application's source, not from memory.

The brief, in the owner's words: "The features we added have made it very heavy for me to even go to this dashboard or refer to it to build things. It just feels chaotic."

This document exists so a designer can see why it feels chaotic — the structure is genuinely doubled right now — and redesign the interface without having to read any code. The redesign is free to move, merge, or delete any screen. It must preserve the operating laws in the last section.

1. What this product is

A private working system for a personal-branding studio (KRNL). One person (the owner, Manmeet) runs content for several brands — some are clients, some are her own. A few clients get read-only logins to see their own work.

It is not a SaaS product with many users. It is one professional's daily workshop, plus a small window each client can look through. It is used every day, mostly on a phone.

2. The core problem a redesign must solve

Two complete navigation systems are live at the same time. An older one (a client list plus a long tab bar) and a newer one (a profile shelf plus three apps). Both work. Both are reachable. Neither has replaced the other. That is the chaos.

5. How it operates — the flow through the system

The intended loop (new model)

Intake→ Curation→ Strategy locked→ Seed→ Costume→ Brief→ Draft→ Gates→ Client review→ Scheduled→ Posted→ Numbers→ Verdict↺ back to seeds

The daily loop (what actually happens most days)

Open Content board→ Make the thing (outside the app: Canva, video editor)→ Preview + send link→ Drag to Scheduled→ Mark posted

A redesign should make the daily loop the fastest path in the product. Today it competes with thirteen tabs for attention.

Two laws that shape every screen

  • Recording is a byproduct, never a chore. No screen may ask her to enter data twice, or to report on work she already did. Moving a card to "posted" is the only reporting act allowed.
  • The client never sees the workshop. Drafts, engine internals, notes, other profiles — none of it reaches a client login, in any switch position.

7. The honest pain list

  1. Two navigation systems at once. Tabs and apps both live. This is the root cause.
  2. 13–15 tabs per profile. No hierarchy: "Brand" (set once a year) sits beside "Content" (used hourly).
  3. No "today" view inside a profile. She lands on a dashboard of counters, not on the work.
  4. The newest tools are the least reachable — Engine and Strategy are tabs 12 and 13.
  5. 78 switches in one flat list with no grouping or defaults surfaced.
  6. Every profile is always visible in the sidebar, so nothing ever feels like one focused world.
  7. Screens that exist for one client only (Orders, Catalogue, Cold Calls, Answers) sit in the same bar as universal ones.
  8. Density. 10–12px type, tight cards, low contrast — heavy to read on a phone.
  9. No sense of progress or state anywhere: nothing shows what needs her next.

8. Rules the redesign must not break

These are operating guarantees, not design preferences. The visual language, layout, hierarchy, and screen count are all free. These are not:
  1. A client login can never see: drafts before review, engine internals, her notes, another profile, or the shelf.
  2. A client can only give in four places: intake answers, assets, review verdicts, and their read on how things are going.
  3. What a profile switches off is not rendered at all — never greyed out, never a locked-looking button.
  4. No screen may show a second copy of a post. One post, one record, many windows.
  5. Nothing may ask her to re-enter something the system already knows.
  6. Analysis must show missing data as missing ("not collecting since 12 July"), never as a zero or a dip.
  7. Phone first. She works from her phone most of the day.

9. What a redesign is being asked for

One interface, not two. A structure where:

  • the daily loop (see what's due → make → preview → schedule → post) is the shortest path in the product;
  • entering a profile feels like entering one world, not switching a filter;
  • rarely-used setup screens (Brand, Strategy, Switches, Intake) are reachable but out of the way;
  • the Engine feels like a room worth sitting in, not tab thirteen;
  • it is calm enough to open on a bad day.

Data can be invented freely in mockups. Screen names can change. Tabs can be deleted or merged. The only fixed points are section 8.

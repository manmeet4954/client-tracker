# Audit — why dashboard work kept breaking

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Asked for by:** Manmeet, after three days of work she described as "just getting broken"
**Scope:** the whole dashboard folder, its docs, its tests, and the live deploy

Filed so this is not re-derived in a future session. Findings 1, 2, 4, 5 and 8
are FIXED as of this date. Findings 3, 6, 7 and 9 are open, and 3 is spec 36.

---

## 1. The same feature was built four times in three days — FIXED (rule added)

The client "add an idea" feature:

- **08-15 night** built as a box inside the client's Intake window
- **08-16 night** that box deleted, rebuilt as a box on the Idea column
- **08-17 morning** that box deleted, rebuilt using her existing Add-post card

Three builds, two deletions, one feature. Each session accepted her correction
and then built a **new** thing rather than reusing the one that existed.

**Root cause:** no spec had been written since 2026-08-08, against her own rule
that anything bigger than a quick fix gets a spec first. Nine days of
client-facing rebuild ran on verbal corrections alone, so the only record of
intent was prose written after the fact by the session that had already
misunderstood.

**Fix:** `CLAUDE.md` rule 0, the plug rule.

---

## 2. "1018 tests, typecheck clean" proves nothing about a screen — FIXED (doc)

The harness has no browser, no React, no DOM. Most tests read source files as
**text** and assert strings appear in them:

```ts
readFileSync('components/shell/Frame.tsx').includes('creation')
```

A screen can be blank, broken or ugly with every test green. Every session
report opened with this number as evidence the work was good. That is the gap
between "done" and the screen she then opened.

**Fix:** `CLAUDE.md` now states plainly what the suite does and does not prove,
and forbids reporting the count as evidence a screen is correct.

The suite is still worth keeping. It is good at catching a deleted rule. It is
not acceptance.

---

## 3. The plug wall — OPEN, this is spec 36

68 of 96 switches are marked `audience: 'owner'`, which vetoes client visibility
in any switch position. Only 29 declarations carry a `client_door`, and a path
without one is invisible even when its switch is on. Making one feature
client-visible means editing four or five files.

This is the mechanical reason every request of hers became a build, and why her
Settings toggles felt fake.

Full analysis and the build plan: `specs/36 — Plug and Play (The Client Sees Her Dashboard).md`

---

## 4. Every client was told their account was paused — FIXED

A client's board is always rendered read-only, and read-only and resting shared
one sentence:

> "Read only. This profile is resting, so nothing here moves."

Every client on a perfectly active profile read that. After the 08-17 change it
would have sat directly above an "Add a post" button.

**Fix:** `boardNote(readOnly, resting)`. Only a genuinely paused, closing or
archived profile may say it is resting.

---

## 5. The overruled rule's fifth home — FIXED

Her rule "you cannot see strategy and analysis until intake is complete" was
overruled on 2026-08-11 and again on 2026-08-17, for clients and for her.

It had five homes. Three were removed on 08-11, a fourth on 08-16. The fifth
was `lib/tree/render.ts` step 4, and it hit **her**: on any profile whose
strategy was not locked, her own Creation, Analysis, Assets, References, Logs
and Platforms dropped to read-only. This is why her own dashboard kept going
quiet on profiles she was mid-way through setting up.

**Fix:** removed, with `AFTER_LOCK_FAMILIES`. `strategy_locked` survives only
for the engine's own generation gate.

---

## 6. Two of everything — OPEN

- **Two route trees:** 23 legacy `app/client/[id]/*` pages and 10 new
  `app/profile/[id]/*` pages, both live.
- **Two data models:** the legacy slices and the path-addressed `body`, both
  live, deliberately per `CLAUDE.md`.

`Board` reads the legacy `contentCards`. `ClientPiecePanel` reads the body
first and falls back to the cards. Each session picks whichever it reads first,
which is a coin flip repeated daily.

Not urgent. Worth a spec of its own before it costs another week.

---

## 7. A client's browser receives all 30 legacy slices — OPEN, folded into spec 36

`filterStateForRole` filters `body` and passes every legacy slice through
untouched. So `brand.strategy` (her private notes), `coldCalls`, `orders`,
`leadAnswers`, `momentum` and `goals` are in the JSON a client downloads.

Nothing draws them, so nothing has been seen. A comment in `ClientWindows.tsx`
claims `brand.strategy` is deliberately excluded, which is true of what is drawn
and false of what is sent.

Not a cross-client leak: `allowedClientIds` is sound and was verified. Fix is
spec 36 §7.

---

## 8. The steering documents had stopped steering — FIXED

- `STATE.md` was 2,556 lines. Its section titled "The single next step" was last
  updated 2026-07-12 and pointed at analytics work five weeks stale.
- `00 — Dashboard Backlog.md`, the stated master list, untouched since 08-04.
- The read order sent a fresh session through ~4,400 lines before starting.

A session following her instructions exactly came out pointed at the wrong work
and had to guess the rest.

**Fix:** `STATE.md` is now 223 lines, current, with her rulings listed and one
next step. The complete history moved to `STATE-ARCHIVE.md` with nothing
deleted.

---

## 9. Deploy status was recorded wrong in both directions — FIXED (procedure)

On 2026-08-17 the live app still ran the one-field idea box she had rejected
that morning, while `STATE.md` claimed the previous night's work was NOT
deployed when it WAS.

So she tested the live app, saw old behaviour, and reported things broken that
were already fixed in the folder. The session checked the code, saw the fix, and
reported it fixed. Both were right about different apps.

**Fix:** `STATE.md` §1 now carries the one command that answers this, and says
to run it rather than trust a sentence.

Also fixed: `DEPLOY.md` had a stale vault path (`Desktop/Manmeet Brain`).

---

## The whole thing in one paragraph

The dashboard had no working definition of "correct". Tests checked for words in
files rather than screens. The state file was too long to read and too stale to
trust. Specs had stopped, so her spoken corrections were the only requirement
and each session heard them fresh. The permission layer was large enough that
any change touched five files. And deploy status was recorded wrong, so she and
the sessions were looking at different apps while discussing the same one. Each
session started blind, built something new, passed tests that proved nothing,
reported success, and handed her a screen she had not asked for.
